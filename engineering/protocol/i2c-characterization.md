# Uno I2C firmware characterization

This reference describes observations of the physical Uno I2C personality reporting `v=0.2.13`, `n=da7e14a9`, `s=5`, `y=0`, `b=s`, and `l=3`, using 57600-baud USB serial and three powered OneWire probes on A0. Program-memory readback matched all 29,288 populated bytes of the prebuilt I2C HEX with SHA-256 `ccf240909702618815864633071e4f1b9e4dba32805241a4f9346b96afdb69ca`; unspecified flash addresses and EEPROM equality are not claimed. These are independently described interoperability facts, not imported firmware implementation or raw fixtures. The owner's setup uses I2C rather than RevC; these observations do not automatically qualify the pinned RevC fixtures or every I2C build.

## LCD degree byte

Repeated `L` reads from this identified I2C artifact contain raw byte `DF` in the degree cells. The separately identified RevC artifact also exports raw byte `B0` without an attached LCD. The emulator exports the selected profile byte unchanged on serial, while its local graphical and terminal adapters render either byte as Unicode `°`.

The regression checks cover both profiles, Celsius and Fahrenheit, configuration/default resets, controller restart, checkpoint continuation, and exact serial LCD bytes. These checks do not qualify physical LCD rendering, rotary behavior, or the nine unresolved RevC characterization cases. No firmware implementation, binary, raw capture, generated table, test, or asset is incorporated into the MIT emulator.

## Duplicate heater pin history

With active-low heater slot 4 on D6, installing a second heater in slot 8 on D10 makes both slot aliases command D10. D6 retains its prior driven level. The four tested histories combine initially inactive/active D6 with removing the older/newer record first. Each history was repeated three times from a restored, restarted baseline, with identical independent input-level sequences. D6→D11 and D10→D12 monitoring passed independent polarity/continuity checks before and after the suite. The existing door-input record was temporarily selected on D11/D12 with noninverted input readback, in test mode; it was restored to inverted D4 during cleanup. These sequential same-board input samples include internal pull-ups and are not calibrated voltage or simultaneous edge measurements.

| History stage | Initially inactive D6 | Initially active D6 | D10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Duplicate installed inactive | HIGH | LOW | HIGH |
| Either slot alias commands heater on/off | Remains HIGH | Remains LOW | LOW/HIGH |
| Newer D10 alias commanded on before removal | HIGH | LOW | LOW |
| Either record removed, surviving alias commanded on, then off | Remains HIGH | Remains LOW | Remains LOW |
| Surviving record removed | Remains HIGH | Remains LOW | Remains LOW |
| Explicit controlling assignments restored inactive | HIGH | HIGH | HIGH |

After the first removal, the surviving record's logical readback can follow its on/off writes while D10 remains physically LOW through the independent input. Neither removal order restores control of the old D6 object. Record deletion and mode transitions were separated; cleanup explicitly reinstalls controlling assignments before driving both outputs inactive. These observations qualify the declared artifact, pins, and histories; they do not establish every duplicate-function or floating-pin outcome. They support the existing F07/F17 compatibility limits and do not promote unrelated unresolved fixtures to passing cases.

The emulator's [slot-query path](../../src/engine.cpp) preserves this fallback read/write behavior when a surviving actuator record has lost its live target, while leaving orphaned pin levels unchanged. The original [native regression](../../tests/model.cpp) replays all four observed I2C histories, including both alias writes, both removal orders, fallback readings, and retained physical-pin state. It failed with a missing `v` before the correction and passes afterward. This covers the observed action/reaction; it does not qualify other duplicate-function histories or the unresolved RevC fixture.

The separated-stage repeat is the evidence for `F17`; retain both observations with their command sequences rather than silently discarding the initial response. ## Shared data loss and embedded discovery diagnostics

With all three installed probes powered and the Arduino-side pull-up retained, interrupting their shared data lead produced one `W2` per ROM in beer/chamber/ambient order. Their installed records remained intact, installed-device values became `null`, `BeerTemp` and `FridgeTemp` became `null`, and `RoomTemp` was omitted. Reconnection produced one `I0` per ROM in the same order and restored live readings. In off mode, the controller remained idle with inactive assigned outputs. During ordinary chamber-constant heating, the same loss forced idle and inactive outputs before the 180-second minimum-on threshold; reconnection entered the ordinary heating wait. These are shared-data observations, not individual-probe interruption, exact electrical latency, or missed-read/reseeding-threshold qualification.

Installing an all-zero ambient ROM and requesting `h{h:2,p:14,v:1,u:0}` reached a different diagnostic path: the failed temporary discovery reader emitted `W2` after the second device object and before the comma introducing the third. The third object retained the zero ROM and had `v:null`; the surrounding `h` list continued after the diagnostic's CRLF. Restoring the original ambient ROM restored its live reading. The [edge regressions](../../tests/edges.py) independently assert the exact embedded bytes with synthetic ROMs and fixed 20 C inputs, without repairing the stream into strict JSON or importing the raw capture. The emulator now emits this observed I2C zero-ROM reply instead of stopping with an unqualified nested-diagnostic exception; the separately observed RevC A4 zero-ROM path emits the same framing with pin 18. The [absent nonzero `00`-prefix case](firmware-findings.md#source-defined-absent-00-prefix-discovery) has separate source-defined coverage. This physical observation does not qualify those nonzero addresses, every wildcard placement, other mismatches, or hardware timing conditions.

The `shared_loss_observation` regression checks the observed diagnostic order, omission/null behavior, retained records, assigned-output shutdown, and heating wait after recovery. It runs both artifact profiles with synthetic inputs remaining at 20 C; it does not claim a numerical match to the uncontrolled physical temperatures or establish when a recovered filter history reseeds. The `nested_discovery_observation` regression checks the reached zero-ROM diagnostic branch, ordinary no-value discovery, and ambient restoration. The [shared-source initialization coverage](firmware-findings.md#sensor-recovery-and-initialization) separately establishes conditional waiting and suspended controller work; exact physical blocking duration and unobserved embedded-diagnostic paths remain unqualified.

## Live beer filter writes, resets, and minimum-on holds

A continuous trace sampled the calibrated unfiltered beer value immediately after each new serial LCD clock witness, together with fast telemetry and the slow-channel `beerDiff` witness under a fixed beer target and zero integral/derivative gains. The emulator matched all 594 exposed fast/slow pairs exactly using one same-tick input alignment; a globally shifted input history and both conventional named-live-write alternatives disagreed. This qualifies the reached `beerFastFilt` saved-only behavior and `beerSlowFilt` live-fast routing in that trace. It does not turn 594 samples into independent trials or qualify phases the trace did not reach. The [dynamics regression](../../tests/dynamics.py) checks these observed live-write invariants under an independently authored synthetic ramp, with a counterexample that ensures the stimulus distinguishes different effective coefficients.

A separate reference sequence reached `beerSlopeFilt:1`, factory `C`, and restart after saved coefficients `1/2/1`. Immediately around `C`, fast and slow values remained distinct from each other and from the unfiltered input; their histories were preserved while the reported live coefficients became factory `3/4/4`. After restart, saved `1/2/1` returned, fast/slow values moved together to a new seed, and slope returned to zero. The original `qualified_filter_reset_history` regression checks these history/readback facts with an independent synthetic step. An independent exact-rational bound nevertheless excludes an unchanged live slow coefficient after the `beerSlopeFilt:1` write: even allowing the unseen input anywhere from 15 to 35 C, either global input phase, and either a skipped or unobserved evaluation, that hypothesis cannot reach the observed 31.309 C slow value. This establishes a live slow-channel effect; it does not qualify the complete three-channel slope-write response. A missing tick witness before that write prevents an exact numerical replay of the entire physical sequence; the 60-sample restarted tail does not qualify effective slope reload before its initial update window. These limitations are separate from the observed reset behavior.

During ordinary heating with sustained opposing stop demand, serial LCD readback showed `Heat time left 00m00`, `T.State=9`, and an active heater readback. The following controller evaluation reported idle, followed by inactive heater readback. The cooling history likewise retained `T.State=8` and an active cooler at `Cool time left 00m00`, followed by idle and an inactive cooler. The native public-command replays preserve both strict greater-than-180 releases, without treating host command time as the controller's timer origin. In the subsequent opposite-demand history, the direction wait ended in `Waiting for peak` with the cooler inactive, and the peak gate later cleared without an `I19` estimator-update diagnostic. Heating missing-peak update arithmetic remains unqualified by that history; waiting through a timeout alone does not qualify the unreached adaptation branch.

## Missing cooling peak

The ordinary cooling history reached `I20` after its pending-peak wait, reporting `20.1`, `19.5`, `20.000`, and `13.320`. The changed estimator read back as `13.32` before and after restart. Every fixed-point estimate consistent with the printed `19.5` gives the same saturated sentinel-error decrease factor, `341/512`; applying it to raw estimator `10240` gives `6820`, or `13.3203125`. Recomputing the error from the replacement current-temperature value would instead give factors `415` through `418`, which the observed update excludes. This qualifies the reached decrease and public persistence without claiming general overflow coverage or an exact physical timeout comparator from host elapsed time. The original `qualified_missing_peak` regression reaches the history through public commands with constant synthetic 20.5 C inputs and checks the diagnostic, pending-peak gate, estimator decrease, and restart readback.

The default modeled I2C door contact is asserted LOW, matching the recorded D4-to-GND active/open bench convention with device inversion `x=1`. This corrects the I2C physical-door/controller disagreement without bypassing inversion, changing serial validation, or changing thermal door state into an electrical-level flag.

## Explicit evidence limits

These regressions qualify the recorded action/reaction above. The earlier interrupted hand-warming trace alone does not qualify live filter routing, settling, or slope response; the continuous trace described above separately qualifies its reached fast/slow write behavior. Other filter, protection-timer, and estimator tests retain their source-defined basis unless a physical observation is explicitly identified; their presence is not proof of unobserved Arduino behavior. No fixed discovery/conversion delay or fabricated embedded diagnostic is introduced from ordinary connected-probe response intervals.

Individual-probe physical loss/recovery traces, floating/shared-pin electrical effects outside the declared model, protection edges finer than the recorded evaluation witnesses, general overflow-sensitive peak arithmetic, physical display/rotary behavior, and memory-sensitive outcomes remain unqualified by this evidence. This does not reopen the reached I2C missing-cooling factor/persistence comparison or the minimum-on boundary histories above. D11/D12 were measurement inputs on the physical bench, not a claim that the emulator models those loopback jumpers. The nine RevC characterization placeholders retain their nonpassing classifications; the [qualification reconciliation](firmware-findings.md#characterization-reconciliation) records their separately covered subclaims, shared-source regressions, and remaining limits. No new physical session is required or implied by this regression suite, and an unobserved case is not reported as passing.
