# Firmware quirks and compatibility limits

Deterministic firmware quirks are required controller behavior. Undefined-memory, overflow, and hardware-dependent outcomes remain explicit compatibility limits; no safer invented serial response substitutes for them. Original findings use MIT; reference source retains its terms.

`D` means a deterministic source-defined expectation with original fixture coverage. `C` means a characterization case whose source bounds/call order are recorded but whose exact runtime result needs hardware/build evidence; no deterministic reply is invented. Physical observations have their own artifact scopes below and in the [I2C evidence register](i2c-characterization.md). A `C` case cannot be called passing or silently rejected by the emulator; qualify it before claiming the affected compatibility. [Fixture semantics](test-vectors/README.md) distinguish these categories.

## Observed sensor-read transitions

On the identified RevC artifact, loss can first be noticed by a serial raw read between ordinary filter evaluations. A `T` reply can retain the cached beer/chamber fast values while its ambient read fails, embedding `D:{...W2...}` directly after `FridgeAnn`, followed by CRLF and the remaining `,"State":...}`. `RoomTemp` is omitted. Subsequent installed `d` reads emit the beer warning immediately after `d:[`, then the chamber warning after the first object and before the second object's comma; those values are null. The ambient warning is not repeated. The next ordinary evaluation does not duplicate these transitions; recovery emits one `I0` per ROM. During an ordinary shared-loss history, the observed order instead follows whichever raw read or ordinary evaluation first detects each failure. The original `read_time_loss_observation` in [edge tests](../../tests/edges.py) asserts the reached cached-value and exact embedded-byte history, while `shared_loss_observation` checks ordinary loss and recovery for both personalities.

Shared DATA interruption in off mode and during heating retained all 16 installed records, omitted ambient telemetry, nullified the required probe readings, and left assigned outputs inactive on both identified artifacts. RevC heating was interrupted less than 40 seconds after its first active witness, and recovery entered state 6 while the heating-off wait still applied. A separate RevC off-mode absence includes 125 distinct failed LCD clock witnesses. Its first recovered beer raw value was 19.5625 C, and fast telemetry immediately read 19.56 C rather than the prior 19.50 C; this records the first-recovery equality and a seed-compatible discontinuity. It does not uniquely identify every hidden pre-loss filter history. The next conversion exposed the hand-warmed probe near 29.7 C while fast values still reflected the recovered seed. Physical raw-read freshness, a universal reconnect delay, and the exact cable-duration mapping to failed reads remain unqualified; the source-defined failed-read threshold, recovery slope-counter phase, and role policies have separate native coverage below. The public-path `observed_revc_long_loss_recovery` regression uses an independently authored small step after a long absence; it does not import physical temperatures or model a fabricated conversion delay.

## Observed RevC filter dynamics

A continuous RevC input/output sequence contains 807 consecutive controller-clock samples within its two restart segments of calibrated beer input, fast telemetry, exposed slow value, and slope, including 330 samples after restart. The public command history writes `beerFastFilt:4`, `beerSlowFilt:4`, `beerSlopeFilt:1`, and `beerSlowFilt:2`, applies factory `C`, and restarts with saved coefficients `4/2/1`. Every exposed fast/slow/slope triplet matches the emulator exactly with DS18B20 input quantization of 1/16 C, one common same-tick input alignment, and no additional startup evaluations before the first sample in either restart segment. There are no missing clock witnesses, per-channel input shifts, interpolated values, or widened numeric tolerances. This qualifies that artifact's reached live routing, `C` coefficient application with retained history, saved restart reload/reseed, and slope response; it does not assert an absolute MCU startup latency or all possible coefficients and inputs.

Defined-prefix counterfactual comparisons disagree when live writes are routed to their named filters, `C` reseeds history, restart reloads factory coefficients or retains history, or slope updates every two or four evaluations. Some counterfactual histories subsequently encounter unsupported fixed-point overflow; only their earlier defined disagreements are evidence, and no wrapped output is invented. The original `qualified_beer_live_writes`, `qualified_filter_reset_history`, `shared_factory_routes`, and `shared_slope_restart` regressions in [dynamics tests](../../tests/dynamics.py) use synthetic ramps and steps to distinguish these behaviors. The I2C evidence remains separately bounded: its fast/slow routing and reset subclaims do not become a complete three-channel numerical qualification merely because RevC matches.

## Observed RevC minimum-on holds and peak outcomes

Under sustained opposing demand, the identified RevC artifact retains an active heater at `T.State=9` and `Heat time left 00m00`, then reports idle with inactive assigned outputs at the next eligible ordinary evaluation. Its pending heating peak subsequently keeps the opposite cooling output inactive in state 7 after the direction wait has matured. The reached `I19` reports `16.6`, `19.1`, `40.000`, and `26.641`; the changed estimate reads back as `26.641` before and after restart.

Every fixed-point estimate consistent with the printed `19.1` gives the sentinel-derived decrease factor `341/512`. Applying it to raw estimator `20480` gives `13640`, exactly `26.640625`. Recomputing error from the replacement current-temperature value would give factors `385` through `388`, excluded by the observed result. The original `qualified_missing_peak` in [dynamics tests](../../tests/dynamics.py) reaches the same heating branch through public commands with constant synthetic 20.5 C inputs and estimator 40, checking the competing peak gate, diagnostic, decrease, and saved reload. This qualifies the reached bounded decrease; it does not qualify general overflow, the I2C heating branch, or an exact physical timeout comparator inferred from host duration.

The cooling hold likewise retains `T.State=8` and an active cooler at `Cool time left 00m00`, followed by idle and inactive assigned outputs. Both profiles' original public-command regressions preserve these observed zero-countdown hold/release histories using synthetic inputs. Physical comparisons qualify the controller-visible boundary history; sub-evaluation phase and calibrated electrical edges are not inferred from the host's request duration.

The RevC cooling candidate reaches a normal `I18` peak instead of missing-peak `I20`, reporting `16.2`, `14.9`, `20.000`, and `15.898`. It therefore does not qualify the RevC missing-cooling-peak branch. The normal observation is retained, and the configured estimator is restored; no second thermal candidate or fabricated timeout pass replaces that outcome. I2C's independently reached `I20` remains scoped to its identified artifact.

## Observed periodic RevC dispatch suspension

The RevC serial stream spanning the hourly display refresh contains a 2.232-second gap between received chunks. A command sent during that gap was answered later, while the controller-visible wait countdown advanced; a short no-byte polling envelope was not a firmware reply. This is consistent with the periodic display reinitialization in the [main loop](https://github.com/brewpi-remix/brewpi-firmware-rmx/blob/8f1fa1213cbbfc1f987fada5168d01925e1a2ee8/src/Brewpi.cpp#L112) and the [RevC driver's mandatory initialization waits](https://github.com/brewpi-remix/brewpi-firmware-rmx/blob/8f1fa1213cbbfc1f987fada5168d01925e1a2ee8/src/SpiLcd.cpp#L47), totaling at least 2,201 milliseconds before additional bus overhead. The previous native instantaneous refresh contradicted the observed dispatch suspension.

The emulator preserves that mandatory RevC wait on its controller clock, deferring serial input and ordinary sensor/PID/output work. Its existing 100-millisecond integration grid first services completion at 2,300 milliseconds; plant/controller time continues and existing output levels remain latched. Completion performs one overdue ordinary evaluation, then processes queued serial bytes in order, without synthesizing missed catch-up evaluations. Checkpoints retain the reset phase and queued input, and an incomplete token receives its ordinary wall-clock timeout after dispatch resumes. The original `periodic_dispatch_pause` [edge regression](../../tests/edges.py) failed before this fix; it checks deferred replies, one resumed PID/sensor evaluation, continuing plant time, and checkpoint continuation. The [process test](../../tests/pacing.py) checks the resumed parser deadline through an isolated PTY.

This is a bounded representation of the reached RevC dispatch suspension, not an exact SPI-wire duration or MCU phase fit to the single received-chunk interval. Additional GPIO/SPI overhead, absolute MCU startup timing, measured I2C display-reset duration, UART behavior, physical LCD rendering, and rotary behavior remain unqualified. Receive histories exceeding the native suspension queue's 64 KiB bound report an explicit local fidelity gap instead of inventing an Arduino overflow reply. No physical display/rotary gate or unresolved memory-sensitive fixture is promoted by these tests.

## Shared control logic and I2C dispatch timing limit

The [RevC and I2C build environments](https://github.com/brewpi-remix/brewpi-firmware-rmx/blob/8f1fa1213cbbfc1f987fada5168d01925e1a2ee8/platformio.ini#L24) share the Uno/AVR target, Arduino framework, and common flags; their environment-specific flag selects the shield. Configuration inspection selects the same cascaded filters, fixed-point formats, controller defaults, coefficient-write routing, persistence paths, timer constants, and peak-learning logic. The different Glycol control branches are selected by neither build. Existing physical observations therefore support the shared algorithms without making every unreached personality-specific comparison a separate requirement for another physical run. Direct artifact qualification remains bounded by the matrix below; common source/configuration does not establish identical historical compiler inputs, peripheral timing, or arbitrary overflow outcomes.

I2C display-reset blocking is represented from source-defined mandatory waits. The common main loop invokes display initialization synchronously. The [display wrapper](https://github.com/brewpi-remix/brewpi-firmware-rmx/blob/8f1fa1213cbbfc1f987fada5168d01925e1a2ee8/src/DisplayLcd.cpp#L60) calls driver initialization and then `begin`; the [I2C initialization path](https://github.com/brewpi-remix/brewpi-firmware-rmx/blob/8f1fa1213cbbfc1f987fada5168d01925e1a2ee8/src/I2cLcd.cpp#L90) also calls `begin`. Each `begin` includes unconditional 50-millisecond and 1,000-millisecond waits before additional initialization work, regardless of whether an LCD acknowledges the bus. The two `begin` calls and final wrapper clear require fixed waits totaling 2,128.3 milliseconds, excluding per-strobe waits, address-scan results, and bus overhead. The emulator rounds this lower bound upward to 2,129 milliseconds; its existing 100-millisecond controller grid first services completion at 2,200 milliseconds. It uses the same dispatch, latched-output, single-overdue-evaluation, and checkpoint behavior as the RevC suspension. The original `periodic_dispatch_pause` [edge regression](../../tests/edges.py) fails on the former instantaneous I2C refresh and checks both profiles; the [process regression](../../tests/pacing.py) checks resumed partial-command deadlines for both. This source-defined suspension can affect serial responsiveness, evaluation timing, and latched-output duration; it does not imply different filter equations. Exact bus overhead, absolute MCU startup phase, and a physical I2C reset-duration comparison remain unqualified. Faithful emulator behavior must preserve the firmware's blocking rather than remove or improve it in the reference firmware.

## Sensor recovery and initialization

The [ordinary sensor-update wrapper](https://github.com/brewpi-remix/brewpi-firmware-rmx/blob/8f1fa1213cbbfc1f987fada5168d01925e1a2ee8/src/TempControl.cpp#L131) attempts an update before reconnecting a backend that still reports disconnected. The failed update increments the absence count without sampling filters or advancing the slope counter; successful initialization seeds stale histories only when the accumulated count exceeds 60 and retains the countdown. The emulator preserves that ordering, including the recovery evaluation's failed read and accumulated short failures. The original `source_sensor_recovery` regression failed against the former immediate recovery sample; it checks both profiles at the count boundary and saturation. The previously expected immediate countdown decrement was a native-test error, not independent Arduino evidence.

The [OneWire initialization path](https://github.com/brewpi-remix/brewpi-firmware-rmx/blob/8f1fa1213cbbfc1f987fada5168d01925e1a2ee8/src/OneWireTempSensor.cpp#L50) conditionally requests a conversion and invokes a [750-millisecond wait](https://github.com/brewpi-remix/brewpi-firmware-rmx/blob/8f1fa1213cbbfc1f987fada5168d01925e1a2ee8/src/OneWireTempSensor.h#L73).
